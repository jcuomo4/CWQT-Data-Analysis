1. Water quality over time, all sites

The top panel shows the typical (median) site's geometric mean each season. The shaded band covers the middle half of sites, and a gray line follows only the long-running sites, so the trend isn't skewed by sites added over the years. The bottom panel shows the share of samples above the single-sample limit of {{LIMIT}}, split into dry and wet weather.

2. Every site, every season

One row per site, grouped by waterbody, and one column per season. Each cell is colored by that season's geometric mean (good, advisory or poor), and gray means the site wasn't sampled. Use it to spot sites that are improving, getting worse or consistently poor. Scroll inside the image to see all sites.

3. How rainfall affects bacteria levels

Samples are grouped by how much rain fell at Central Park on the sampling day and the two days before. Heavy rain overwhelms the combined sewer system and sends sewage-contaminated overflow into the waterways, so bacteria levels and the share of samples above {{LIMIT}} rise steadily with rainfall.

4. Recent water quality by waterbody ({{RECENT_RANGE}})

Each row is a waterbody, ordered from cleanest to most contaminated over the last {{RECENT_SEASONS}} seasons. The large dot is the geometric mean of all samples in that waterbody, and small dots are its individual sites.

5. The latest 30 days ({{WINDOW}})

Each site's geometric mean over the most recent 30 days of sampling, ranked from cleanest to most contaminated. This is the same calculation as the 30-day geometric mean tabs in the CWQT master sheet. Hollow dots mark sites with fewer than {{MIN_N}} samples in the window.

6. Full time series for each site

Every regular-season sample at a site, season by season, with dry- and wet-weather samples marked and each season's geometric mean. Choose a site below, or download all sites in one PDF.
